# TennisBall browser game

Play: https://SimoneStraccia1987.github.io/TennisBall-web/

Sign in, click Start, then choose Training for the wave campaign, Match for tennis versus AI, or Padel for local doubles with one human and three AI players.
Training: complete or skip the tutorial, then use the ground aim circle with mouse, touch, swipe, keyboard or gamepad. It starts at the centre of the court half across the net. Put the circle under a target for a direct hit, or inside its highlighted bounce area to bounce on the ground before hitting it. Bounce areas on your side or crossing the net stay hidden. Click, tap/release or use the right trigger to confirm; Auto-launch uses your ground selection when ready. Every wave includes a low target with a visible amber bounce choice. Targets stay below 1.4 m and at least 1 m from the net. The three practice levels progress from central placement to lateral recovery and varied match preparation. Q toggles lob, D sprints, and F charges a power shot.
Training and Match feature directional tennis footwork, height-adjusted forehand/backhand swings and animated serve toss, contact and recovery. Automatic movement respects physical reach, so some returns remain out of range.
While approaching a launch zone, the player opens a forehand or backhand based on running direction and holds that pose while the legs keep moving. At the contact stance, only the shorter final stroke remains. Inside a ready zone, WASD/left-stick movement controls aim and queued free movement stops; movement resumes when readiness ends.
When several return zones are close, manual movement acquires the nearest physically reachable one. The character then automatically finishes its contact stance while the same touch stick, keyboard/gamepad or pointer controls aim. The choice stays fixed until the contact ends; canceling the touch finger does not cancel positioning. Right-click/double-tap navigation presses become aim motion without shooting. Expired or unreachable contacts cannot acquire movement ownership.
Both modes show up to nine incoming launch zones: low, medium and high contacts before the bounce, rising after it, and falling after it. Only physical opportunities are displayed, as the external contour of their combined ground circles. Internal outlines, height labels, vertical contact indicators and floating player/target health bars are removed; health, damage and healing remain active. Forehand/backhand preparation now plays in the upper body while the lower body continues directional movement; footwork blends into the full stroke before contact. Low returns retain a lower before-bounce contact. Colors run from green to amber to red as reaching a zone gets harder from your current position. Human and AI base movement is 3.5 m/s; player upgrades and Sprint remain available. Flight prediction continues beyond contact through the second ground bounce, regardless of faults, court boundaries or time settings. Rebound, friction and spin response have been corrected and calibrated against the standard tennis-ball drop test.
Match: play one short singles set to four games, win by two, with a seven-point win-by-two tie-break at 4-4. Games use deuce and advantage; servers alternate and players change ends. Serve diagonally into the service box; a first fault gives a second serve, double faults lose the point, and service lets repeat the same attempt. Return serves after the bounce. The regulation-size hard court, visible rackets, overhead serves and full-court framing use the existing physics and animation systems. Choose a landing point with mouse/touch or arrows/right stick; hit with Enter/RT or click/tap. Settings pauses and offers Restart/Menu. Shared settings support automatic/manual movement, swipe aim, auto launch and aim assistance. Sprint uses D/Shift or A; PowerShot uses F/Space or X according to movement mode. Q/B arms a lob. The AI uses bounded footwork and alternates centre/open-side placements. Both modes share HUD, settings and camera choices.

Padel: choose Padel 2 vs 2, then Easy, Normal or Hard to start. Back returns to mode selection; Restart keeps your chosen difficulty. Play on the enclosed 10 x 20 metre court using the same movement, aiming and timed swing controls. The scoreboard tracks doubles service order, points, games, sets and tiebreaks; win two sets. A serve drops and bounces before the racket strikes it. Return the serve after its diagonal bounce; later volleys and legal glass rebounds remain playable. Enable Frame player and target to fit all four players, the ball and active launch zones using the selected camera. Settings offers pause, restart and return to menu.

The 3D game canvas fills the browser viewport. All game controls use Godot UI; the web host has no toolbar or overlays.
With Touch stick movement enabled, press anywhere on the playable screen and drag to move. Release and press anywhere again to start a fresh stick. When you reach the serve ball or a ready launch zone, the same visible stick stops movement and controls aim; drag to aim, then release to hit. Predicted zones alone do not enable aiming. Leaving readiness cancels aim and further drag resumes movement. Settings and skill controls keep their own touch input. Settings can be scrolled vertically from sliders as well as cards; horizontal slider drags and taps adjust values.
Tap the Sprint card to sprint. Hold Power Shot to charge, then release to arm the next shot; opening Settings or leaving the game cancels an unfinished charge.
On iPhone, use Share > Add to Home Screen > Add and launch TennisBall from its icon.
On Android, use the browser menu > Install app or Add to Home screen.
Home Screen app mode hides browser toolbars; system status/navigation UI may remain.

This repository contains the exported browser game. The Godot C# source project is maintained separately.
The browser runtime uses Godot 4.7.2 and 2dog on .NET WebAssembly / WebGL 2.
GitHub Pages publishes the main branch root. Keep .nojekyll and the _framework directory.
